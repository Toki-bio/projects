About

LF-SINE (SINEbase entry LF, Bejerano 2006; Repbase LFSINE_Vert from Latimeria) was searched with a series of consensus queries in several dozen vertebrate and invertebrate genomes between July 2024 and March 2025. This card collects what the files on KIT show: where each query came from, how many hits each genome gives with the same query, what the orthology runs found, which earlier statements had to be corrected, and where the data are. Counts are lines of search hit files, not curated copy numbers; only counts made with the same query and thresholds are comparable.

Derived queries in cartilaginous and ray-finned fishes

chim.q finds far more than lfsine.cons in cartilaginous fishes: sharks 2,510 (whale shark) to 224,693 (Squalus acanthias), Pristiophorus 182,188, Heptranchias 151,191, white shark 100,399; chimaeras 12,900-17,117; rays 2,779-4,932; sawfish 11,645.

fish.q finds 3,108 hits in the sterlet and 2,293 in the paddlefish. The previously known shark and chimaera SINEs (CM-1, HE1, SacSINE1) show no similarity to lfsine.cons (E > 0.07).

Birds: dnaPipeTE and searches

In the dnaPipeTE repeat tables (annotated components of 200 bp or more) LF-SINE accounts for 0.616% of annotated reads in moa (102 SINE-sized components), 0.015% in emu, 0.005% in kiwi, 0.002% in tinamou, and none in ostrich and chicken. The files of the five other species are filtered differently from the moa file; the comparison was redone on equal footing and the enrichment holds. Sequencing depth per species cannot be checked from the files.

Searches with lfsine.q in 2024: emu assemblies 392-526 hits, cassowary 495, rhea 390, kiwi 372, golden eagle 406, buzzard 401, tinamou 220, chicken 216.

Orthology: lungfish against coelacanth

The SINE_orth_loc run Neoceratodus against Latimeria (LFSINE.q) could anchor only 8 loci out of about 15,000 candidates (14,127 failed on the left flank). Six of the eight 'shared' loci are the coelacanth against its own unplaced scaffolds (assembly duplicates), so the number of shared lungfish-coelacanth loci is zero. The two real pairs show a full-length LF-SINE in the coelacanth at an orthologous site where the lungfish has none. The control run, two coelacanth assemblies against each other, shares 115,789 loci (113 differences).

No orthology-based evidence for a shared insertion between lungfish and coelacanth was found. With the same query LFSINE-rb.q the coelacanth has 114,793 hits and the Australian lungfish 5,183, the other two lungfishes 15 and 0.

Corrections made during the audit

  • ZJU2.0 is the emu genome, not chicken; 'penguin' was Rhea pennata; the RepeatMasker comparison was done in emu.
  • The path search 'lfsine' also matched 59,147 SINE_orth_loc result files (*.lfSINE): Lagomorpha, Squamata, Tal and GekkoSINE are not LF-SINE folders.
  • A '4,173-hit cluster' in the beluga flank clustering was an artifact of counting blank separator lines; the real largest cluster has 282 hits.
  • The 6 'shared' orthologous loci of the lungfish run are coelacanth against coelacanth.
  • Counts made with different queries (lfsine.q, LFSINE-rb.q, lfsine.cons) were being compared; the tables now separate them.
  • In the desman assemblies LFSINE-rb.q gives about 3,400 short partial hits (66% identity over about 114 bp to lfsine.cons): probably another tRNA-derived SINE, not evidence for LF-SINE.
